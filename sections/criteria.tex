\section{Research Methodology and Selection Criteria}
To address the research question regarding the evolution of cyber deception from static honeypots to AI-driven systems,
this study adopts a systematic approach to literature selection.
The review process was guided by strict inclusion criteria and a rigorous quality assessment strategy to balance foundational
authority with cutting-edge relevance.

\subsection{Search Strategy and Scope}
The literature search was primarily conducted using the Google Scholar database,
which serves as a comprehensive index for major academic repositories including IEEE Xplore,
ACM Digital Library, and USENIX Association proceedings. The search strings combined core terms such as ``Cyber Deception'',
``Honeypots'', and ``Moving Target Defense'' with emerging keywords like ``Reinforcement Learning'', ``LLM'', ``Generative AI'',
and ``Game Theory''. The time window spans from foundational works (2002) to the most recent developments (early 2025).
\subsection{Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria}
The selection process applied the following criteria:
\begin{itemize}
      \item \textbf{Inclusion Criteria:}
            \begin{itemize}
                  \item Papers proposing novel deception architectures, taxonomies, or evaluation metrics.
                  \item Studies explicitly integrating AI, Game Theory, or Cognitive Psychology into deception strategies.
                  \item Recent experimental reports on Large Language Models (LLMs) used for both offensive
                        and defensive deception purposes.
            \end{itemize}
      \item \textbf{Exclusion Criteria:}
            \begin{itemize}
                  \item Papers lacking technical implementation details or theoretical depth.
                  \item Duplicate studies or minor variations of previously published works.
                  \item Sources not written in English.
            \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Source Quality Assessment and Hierarchy}
In response to the rapid pace of innovation in this domain, this review incorporates sources across three distinct quality tiers,
applying different levels of critical scrutiny to each:

\begin{enumerate}
      \item \textbf{Tier 1: Foundational and Top-Tier Venues (Gold Standard).} \\
            Core concepts and definitions are derived exclusively from high-impact journals
            \footnote{Journal impact metrics verified via LetPub Journal Selector: \url{https://apa.letpub.com/journal-selector}}
            and top-tier conferences (CORE A*/A)
            \footnote{Conference rankings verified via the CORE Conference Portal: \url{https://portal.core.edu.au/conf-ranks/}}.
            These include \textit{USENIX Security} for seminal honeypot frameworks and cognitive efficacy studies,
            \textit{ACM Computing Surveys} for taxonomic definitions, and \textit{IEEE Transactions on Information Forensics and Security
                  (TIFS)} for validated defensive architectures. These sources are treated as the authoritative baseline for this review.

      \item \textbf{Tier 2: Emerging Research and Pre-prints.} \\
            Given the explosive growth of Generative AI in 2023-2025, traditional peer-review cycles often lag behind current threats.
            Consequently, this review includes recent pre-prints (e.g., arXiv) specifically to cover LLM-driven threats
            and generative honeypots. These sources are identified as preliminary and are evaluated based on their experimental
            reproducibility rather than peer-review status.

      \item \textbf{Tier 3: Specialized and Contextual Contributions.} \\
            Selected works from lower-ranked venues or broad-scope journals are included only when they provide specific artifacts
            not available elsewhere. The contributions from these sources are critically analyzed
            and cross-referenced with higher-tier literature to mitigate potential validity concerns.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Limitations}
It is acknowledged that the inclusion of non-peer-reviewed pre-prints and Tier 3 sources introduces a risk of lower rigor.
To mitigate this, findings from these sources are presented as ``emerging trends''
or ``proposed methods'' rather than consolidated facts, pending further validation by the community.
